**CHAPTER 1: JOY SPREADS BENEATH THE SOMBER MOON**

*Tick\~*

A full moon hung in a night sky without a single star.

It looked down over a bustling street in Asterfall.

Stalls sold drinks and turmeric-marinated meat skewers glowing red over
charcoal, with clothing booths lining the roadside. Along both sides of
the street, groups of people walked and talked, shoulders brushing in
the crowd. Every so often, someone would look up at the bright full moon
above the rooftops. Beyond them, the drooping willow forest lay washed
in pale silver-white moonlight, magnificent yet touched by something
strangely mournful.

One child held a bubble wand, while another group of children ran around
playing blind man\'s bluff.

Amid the lively mix of voices and footsteps filling the street, some
stalls had already put out little gifts of affection for people to give
their loved ones at the upcoming Moonwatch Festival. A girl dressed in
black velvet reached out and picked up a letter:

\- \"Heyyy Eda, this is so pretty. I\'m getting so excited\... too bad I
don\'t have enough money, huh.\" - Atom M.

The girl in green, about a handspan taller than her, gave a little
snort:

\- \"Yep, gonna buy it for me? Haha\" - Eda.

\- \"Oh\... That\'s not what I meant.\" - Atom M.

The vendor kept staring at the black crown on Atom\'s head, covered in
gemstones that shifted faintly as she moved. Then he looked over at Eda:

\- \"Who are you two cosplaying as? Are you part of the cast for the
Moonwatch Festival show? Looking great.\" - The vendor sounded excited.

For a moment, an odd silence settled over them. Atom shot him a sidelong
look. Eda placed a hand on her shoulder:

\- \"Ay, calm down. I know you better than anyone.\" - Eda.

Without another word, Atom tossed the letter aside. She grabbed Eda by
the hand and pulled her toward a nearby ice-cream stand. The owner, a
long-bearded man in a white shirt, was handing an ice cream to a
pregnant woman leading a four-year-old child beside her: \"Here, eat up.
Tired already?\". The man behind the counter looked straight at the two
girls.

\- \"Ice cream, ladies?\" - The man.

\- \"Ohhh. Two strawberry cones.\" - Eda.

In the middle of the street, a notice board was covered in pinned sheets
of paper. A strong gust tore away one page that looked old and crumpled.
Three students, boys and girls, stood in front of the board. One of the
boys picked up a sheet that still looked brand-new, with a large title
printed across the top: \"RED SKY LECTURE\".

\"The Asterfall Government is pleased to announce a lecture on the Red
Sky Phenomenon, to be held during the Moonwatch Festival three weeks
from now. Content prepared by: *Researcher Bezsmyslenoe*.\"

The full moon still shone overhead, casting hazy gray light over the
treetops. The willow leaves reflected it like little mirrors, making the
whole forest shimmer with an almost unreal glow. Beyond the drooping
willows, far from the city, stood a row of lodging rooms tucked deep
behind the forest.

\"Clack clack clack.\"

In the quiet room, only the chirping of cicadas drifted in through the
window. Why did the wind keep striking the pane with one corner already
broken away?

The steady sound of typing.

\"Clack clack.\"

\"Hmmmm\... The Shadow of Reality: A Novel; The Moon and the Pain of
Nostalgia;\... Are these really worth researching?\" - ???

A young man sat on a plastic chair, typing quickly on his laptop, deeply
absorbed in whatever was on the screen. The clock ticked on, each beat
somehow sounding like a hammer driving a nail into a wooden board,
splitting the strange stillness of the night.

He glanced out the window at the trees swaying and rustling in the
distance. Only for a moment. Then his eyes returned to the white screen.
The laptop sat on a wooden desk with a few books tucked into the narrow
shelves around it. He quickly pulled out a notebook, little more than a
cover stuffed with loose sheets of paper.

\- \"Astronomer Pernes\...?\"

Two or three more pages turned softly.

\- \"Aajhon, M.A. in Ancient History\... I\'ll start with these two
tomorrow.\"

The young man scratched at his temple with one finger, brushing past a
few beads of sweat running down his cheek. Then someone knocked on the
door behind him. Knock knock knock.

\- \"Hey, Bezs. Hey. C'mere, I'm gonna tell you something.\" - Eda\'s
voice came from outside.

Bezsmyslenoe\... pushed his chair back, its legs scraping harshly across
the floor. He hurried to the door. Wearing an oversized gray shirt and
pink boxer shorts covered in little cats, he reached for the handle and
opened it.

\- \"Hahaha, nice shorts dude. Come hang out at Atom\'s.\" - Eda.

\- \"Uh\... Go ahead. I\'ll be there in a bit\...\" - Bezs paused, a
little embarrassed.

He watched Eda run farther down the hallway, let out a long breath, then
gently closed the door. His laptop screen still glowed in a room with
hardly a sound. His eyelids felt ready to fall shut when he looked at
the messy stack of papers on his desk.

\- \"Haah\... Am I just going to keep living like this\...?\" - Bezs.

The cicadas outside filled the room, while the second hand of the clock
ticked as though it were trying to say something. He stood still for a
few more seconds, then wandered over to the metal wardrobe in the corner
and pulled it open with a faint creak. Clothes hung from a metal bar
across the top, while almost nothing below was neatly arranged. Bezs
crouched and rummaged through the folded clothes piled underneath, until
his fingers seemed to catch on the sharp metal edge of something hidden
deep beneath them.

His hand stopped. He stared into the dark space inside the wardrobe as
if trying to remember what he had been doing. Bezs gripped the thing
buried beneath the clothes, something he no longer even remembered
hiding there, and pulled out an old photograph, its ink slightly faded
and blurred. It showed a woman with a gaunt face, her hair tied up with
one loose strand hanging down as though she had not bothered to brush it
back. She was forcing a small smile for the camera. Beside her stood a
boy in a white shirt, holding a treasured certificate in both hands.

\- \"Oh\... how long has it been? I can't believe I forgot this was
here.\"

He meant to put the photograph straight back into the corner of the
wardrobe, but his hands stopped halfway. The woman in the faded, grayish
picture seemed to be smiling at him. For a moment, his chest tightened.
His thumb brushed over the bent corner of the photograph.

\- \"I don\'t know why this world has to be so strange\...\"

The phone on his desk lit up with a ding from a messaging app. Bezs kept
staring at the photo, then suddenly remembered something. He pulled open
the drawer beneath the pile of clothes, quickly put the photograph
inside, and pushed it shut again.

. . .

\- \"I\'m gonna find you soon. A ha ha.\" - A boy on the TV had a white
cloth tied over his eyes.

Bam! On the TV, a black monster covered in eyes suddenly lunged through
a window. Three or four people screamed at once\...

\- \"Oh my god, look at them screaming.\" - Atom M.

\- \"What a stupid movie. None of this makes any sense haha.\" - Eda.

Knock knock. Someone knocked on the door. Atom looked toward it, one arm
draped around Eda beside her while her other hand picked up the remote
and switched off the TV. The screen gave a faint burst of static, then
went silent. Another girl in green sat at the kitchen table with a
half-finished bag of chips. She brushed the crumbs from her fingers and
walked over to open the door.

As soon as it cracked open, Bezs was standing outside, his hair still a
little messy, now wearing something neater than before.

\- \"Oh, welcome.\" - Eda at the door.

At the same time, another voice called out from the sofa behind her.

\- \"Come in. What're you doing out there, watching birds?\" - Eda on
the sofa.

Bezs only glanced at the girl in green in front of him, then looked past
her toward the Eda sitting beside Atom. He did not seem surprised.
Scratching lightly at one cheek, he stepped inside.

\- \"Oh wow\... There\'s a lot of you today.\" - Bezs

\- \"What? Is this new to you?\" - Eda on the sofa laughed.

The other Eda closed the door behind him, casually crushed the
potato-chip bag in one hand, then walked with Bezs toward the dining
table beside the kitchen.

He pulled the laptop from under his arm and set it on the dining table,
not far from the sofa. The white glow from the screen lit his tired face
as it opened. Across the room, Atom pushed herself up from the chair.
Eda, who had been sitting beside her on the sofa, also got up and
started following, only to turn halfway toward the refrigerator beside
the kitchen.

Bezs moved the mouse across the table and typed a few words.

\- \"Um\... I actually came over to ask you guys something.\" - Bezs.

Atom pulled over a nearby chair and sat across from the laptop, leaning
slightly toward the many document tabs still open on the screen.

\- \"Oh? What is it\" - Atom M.

\- \"I\'m planning to go talk to these two tomorrow. I kinda want
someone to come with me\... It\'s hard to ask questions and take notes
at the same time. If I miss something, at least someone else might catch
it. And\..."

Bezs paused for a moment, looked toward Eda by the refrigerator, then
back at Atom.

\- \"\...going alone's boring too.\" - Bezs.

The Eda standing by the refrigerator had taken out a large pitcher of
iced tea, its sides still covered in tiny beads of condensation. She
took four glasses from the cabinet above and set them on the counter one
by one with small clinks.

The Eda sitting on the chair looked up at him.

\- \"Could have just said that first.\"

Atom rested her chin on one hand, her eyes still fixed on the screen,
tiny lines of text reflected in her dark pupils. Behind her came the
trickle of tea pouring into each glass, followed by the light clink of
ice as Eda tipped the pitcher.

\- \"Mm. But I\'m busy tomorrow. Gotta go back to the old place.\" -
Atom M.

\- \"The old place\...?\" - Bezs looked up.

She said nothing more.

Bezs watched Atom for another moment, then simply nodded and turned back
to the laptop. A glass of tea was set down beside his hand. Eda pushed
another toward Atom, another toward herself where she sat nearby, while
keeping the last glass in her hand as she stood behind the Eda in the
chair. The pitcher was placed in the middle of the table, condensation
gathering into a small ring beneath it.

\- \"What about you?\" - Bezs turned toward the seated Eda.

\- \"Umm\...\" - Eda paused, the potato chip in her hand not yet
reaching her mouth. - \"I\'m busy tomorrow too.\"

Atom glanced sideways at her.

\- \"Huh? You? Busy?\"

\- \"I've got stuff. Important stuff. Don\'t ask.\" - Eda nodded firmly.

Bezs and Atom kept staring at her

Eda put the potato chip into her mouth, crunched away, then looked back
at both of them as if nothing was wrong.

\- \"Well then\...\" - Bezs.

The Eda behind them took two loud gulps of tea and lowered the glass a
little. The Eda in the chair pointed her thumb toward the Eda standing
behind her.

\- \"But my shadow can go with you, Bezs.\"

Bezs looked toward the girl standing behind them. The Eda behind the
chair, halfway through raising the glass to her mouth, suddenly froze.
Her eyes shifted down toward Bezs and Atom, then toward the Eda sitting
in front of her.

\- \"But\... but I\'m busy too?\" - Eda with the glass of iced tea.

\- \"Trust me, she's going with you tomorrow. Hahahha.\" - Eda in the
chair gave a little laugh.

Bezs pulled the laptop a little closer to the center of the table,
apparently deciding not to ask any more questions.

\- \"Um\... okay.\"

He opened the note from earlier. Two names stood out among a mess of
addresses, times, and several question marks he did not even remember
adding.

\- \" First, we\'re going to Astronomer Pernes\'s office. He\'s been
observing Asterfall\'s sky for years. I found a few old records with his
name on them about the sky changing color around full moons. \" - Bezs
pointed at the line on the screen. - \" If I can ask him about it, we
might get enough information\... to see whether the Red Sky actually
follows some kind of cycle. \"

Eda, sitting beside Atom, took a sip of tea and reached for another
chip. Bezs moved his finger farther down.

\- \" And this is Aajhon. He has a master\'s in Ancient History. He
might know more about the world\'s history and old legends.\"

\- \"Where are they?\" - Atom asked.

\- \"Pernes is right here in the city, see?\" - Bezs turned the screen
slightly, pointing at the map open beside the note. - \"Aajhon is
farther away, but we should still be able to walk there.\"

Atom slowly turned her gaze toward the window.

\- \" If you\'re going all the way out to Aajhon, stop by the temple in
the forest on the way back. \"

Bezs\'s finger stopped on the mousepad.

\- \"The Moon Temple? In the Aszha Forest?\"

\- \" Deep in the forest. It\'s kinda old. There should be plenty of
records in there nobody\'s looking after. Might be something useful for
you?\"

Bezs sat still and thought for a moment, then reached for the small
notebook beside him and flipped through two or three pages until he
found some space. He quickly wrote something down.

\- \"You\'ve been there before? What\'s inside\...?\" - Bezs.

\- \"A temple\...\" - Atom looked at him.

Bezs scribbled a few more lines beneath tomorrow\'s route, drew a line
from Aajhon to the words \"Moon Temple?\", then scratched at his temple
with one finger while calculating how long it would take to travel
between all three places.

Behind Atom, Eda was still drinking her tea, one hand holding the glass
close to her mouth. Her other hand suddenly settled on the shoulder of
the Eda sitting in front of her. A thin green light flickered between
her fingers and spread across the standing Eda\'s body. Fine lines of
static ran through her, starting from the hand on the seated Eda\'s
shoulder and crawling up her arm, across her green clothes, then
flickering over her face. Her body blurred like an image losing its
signal for a moment, then slowly disappeared. The glass, still held just
in front of her mouth, dropped.

\"Crash!!\"

Cold tea splashed across the floor. A few ice cubes rolled under the
table with little clacks, while shards of glass slid a short distance
before coming to rest, glittering beneath the ceiling light. Atom
snapped her head toward the Eda sitting beside her.

\- \"Uhh, why didn\'t you just put the glass down first?\"

Eda froze, still holding a chip between her fingers.

\- \"Oops, I forgot.\" - Eda scratched lightly at her cheek. - \"I\'m
kinda sleepy.\"

Atom closed her eyes and let out a heavy breath, then pushed her chair
back and stood. She walked toward a small corner beside the kitchen and
reached for something propped against the wall. Bezs leaned down
slightly to look at the glass near his feet and quickly lifted both of
them a little off the floor.

Knock knock. Someone was at the door.

Bezs, still absorbed in his screen and notebook, turned his head. Atom
remained by the kitchen corner, not yet doing anything about the tea
spreading across the floor. She did not bother walking over, only called
out toward the door:

\- \"Ray. Come in.\"

The handle slowly turned downward from the other side, followed by a
soft click. A young man stood in the hallway, dressed in a neat dark-red
suit. His dark blond hair fell close to his collar. A thorned crown
rested on his head, its sharp points woven through his hair. The yellow
hallway light caught the metal and made it glow a dark red. A faint red
heat shimmer radiated from his clothes and spread across the wall beside
him.

The man in red stood at the doorway, his eyes sweeping over Atom, Eda,
then Bezs sitting in front of the laptop.

\- \"Um. May I use the restroom? Bezs\'s room is locked.\" - Ray.

The room, noisy only a second ago, somehow fell completely silent. Atom
was still standing beside the kitchen corner. Eda, a potato chip in
hand, looked toward Ray at the door. Ray walked straight inside, passing
the dining table where the pitcher of iced tea was still beaded with
condensation. His shoes tapped softly against the floor.

The man in red went farther inside, pulled open the bathroom door with
one hand, and stepped in. The door closed behind him.

The clock on the wall ticked for what felt like a very long time, as
though some stretch of time had quietly passed.

Outside the window, the willow branches kept swaying back and forth in
the night. The wind sounded like the lullaby of a mother longing for her
child, a soft, gentle song brushing against the window frame in slow
beats. Overhead, a thin cloud drifted across the moon, covering its
light for a few moments before the wind pushed it away again. Somewhere,
the hands of a clock kept moving, minute after minute passing through a
night that seemed no different from any other.

. . .

\"Click.\"

The door to Bezs\'s room was locked.

He sat on the edge of the bed, shoulders slightly slumped, his shirt a
little wrinkled from being tugged at several times. The laptop lay
closed on his desk, beside the same messy stack of papers and the
notebook he had brought back from Atom\'s room.

Bezs raised both hands and rubbed his eyes hard, then slowly lowered
them.

\- \"I\'m exhausted\...\"

The cicadas outside still droned on, sounding near and far with each
gust slipping through the broken corner of the window. The thin curtain
beside it puffed out slightly, then fell still again. Moonlight slipped
through the opening and painted a pale strip across the floor.

Bezs remained seated on the bed for a while, both hands braced on his
knees. His eyes moved from the laptop on the desk to the metal wardrobe
beside it..

For a brief moment, he remembered the photograph he had put away in the
drawer earlier. His eyelids lowered slightly.

Bezs slowly leaned back, pulled one corner of the blanket across his
stomach, and kept staring up at the ceiling\... Tomorrow, Pernes first.
Then Aajhon. If there was still enough time, the temple in the forest.

His brow tightened faintly. His mind was still trying to sort through
the places and questions in no particular order, even though his eyelids
had grown so heavy he could barely keep them open. His vision gradually
blurred beneath the light coming in from outside. Once again, the room
sank into silence with nothing but the cicadas and the wind.

The full moon outside was still awake in the night, like a sorrowful eye
watching the weary young researcher stretched out on his bed.
Gray-silver light spilled across the floor. As if someone kept asking:
Why must I feel so sad on a night like this\...?
