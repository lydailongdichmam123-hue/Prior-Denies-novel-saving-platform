The memory of Bezs’s late mother makes him want to cry for having no one left to rely on, yet at the same time cling desperately to these fleeting moments. That sweet lullaby, just like back then, pours into his ears like streams of honey, warming the burdens and sorrows within him.

The space is gray and dim, the ceiling light both bright and flickering inside the simple wooden house. Beside the fireplace, the flame trembles weakly, pale and faint, yet still enough to preserve a sense of distant, familiar nostalgia. The silence of the desolate room amplifies the echo of the lullaby, resonating throughout the house. He can hear every breath of his mother, every heartbeat of his own. The old paintings on the wall... dark wooden frames, their images faded away at some unknown point. From the window, the rain pours steadily outside, each drop striking the roof like claws scraping against tightly nailed wooden planks.

Thunder rumbles somewhere in the distance. Lightning tears across the window, flooding the room with a cold white light before fading again.

The horror jolts Bezs awake. In his delirium, he moves his lips, the bitter and metallic taste still lingering. His eyes and throat still burn with an unbearable stench. He suddenly feels emptiness beneath his feet — looking down, the Aszha forest shrinks beneath him; he is flying, moving rapidly through the sky.

Deep within the drooping willow branches, a mass of dense black matter writhes forward, glitching, flickering like something constantly breaking apart and stitching itself back together, covered in countless crimson eyes. It surges forward violently beneath a gray sky filled with dark clouds. The sun has already been devoured by a shadow of the moon, leaving only a faint silver halo casting cold light over the forest. Bezs feels weightless as he is carried swiftly through the air.

The one holding his arm — Ray... breathing heavily, exhausted, panicked, desperately flying to escape the thing chasing them below. It rises and sinks, stretches and retracts, constantly glitching. Distorted static noises echo from somewhere, even from far distances.

Bezs looks closely — Ray’s eyes have turned pitch black. Dark veins spread from his eye sockets, and rotten tears stream down his face. Despite his magnificent crimson wings and the blazing scythe trailing behind him, nothing can conceal the ruin within him; he has clearly been locked in a fierce battle with that entity for a long time.

A suffocating pressure fills the air. The black liquid tries to spill from Bezs’s mouth again. The static noise blends with the screams of thousands, surrounding both of them in the sky.

A rectangular red mirror opens before them. Ray gathers his strength and flies straight into it — through endless darkness, into a twisted, burning corridor within a multidimensional mirror. In just seconds, they emerge in a dark alley of the bustling city of Bharrat.

The street is alive — crowded, filled with food stalls and glowing lights. Ray nearly collapses from exhaustion, his face pale and lifeless, while Bezs remains shaken.

- “Haiz... that was close,” - Ray.

The dark streaks remain around Ray’s eyes. Bezs still stares, dazed, recalling the entity from the forest... could that truly be the angel from the ritual book?

Suddenly, Bezs’s stomach growls loudly. Ray looks at him seriously with those pitch-black eyes. Bezs feels a flicker of embarrassment, but exhaustion overwhelms him: “What? I threw everything up... I’m just a bit hungry now...”

They walk out of the dark alley into the crowded street, illuminated by the vibrant energy of city life. A child is led by their mother past a beef stall. Above the 35-story skyscraper, the golden sunset lingers quietly.

A young man passes by: “Nice scythe, bro. Where’d you get the LED? That cosplay’s insane!” He gives a thumbs-up and walks away, leaving Ray staring blankly with a murderous gaze in his dark eyes.

An old woman holding a basket with a white cat and fresh vegetables walks by. Seeing their disheveled state and smelling the stench clinging to them, she sighs: “Oh dear... poor kids, dressed like that... must be performers not getting paid properly, huh?” She drops an old coin in front of Ray and leaves them in stunned silence.

His throat still burns; his eyes still sting from the aftermath. As they walk, Bezs recounts everything to Ray — from the Aszha forest to the angel and the dream during his coma. Ray’s expression grows increasingly grim.

- “Actually, that thing... is Ledeniel. I cannot resolve his madness,” - Ray.

- “That black mass was Ledeniel? No way... And your eyes...?” - Bezs stares at the dark streaks still spreading from Ray's sockets.
