These were rare days of peace for Bezs; for a researcher, working in such a relaxed state of mind felt like a quiet luxury. With a month left until the Moon-viewing Festival, there wasn’t really any need to rush his thesis on the red sky phenomenon that occurs every 500 years. But where to begin...

He thought about visiting the Moon Shrine, deep within the Aszha Forest to look for relevant documents. But for now… maybe a nap first.

Early the next morning, as dawn had only just begun to break, the sunlight was gentle and clear, a quiet sense of peace settling into every breath. Bezs, along with his belongings… and Eda, made their way into the Aszha Forest. The lush canopy above, with rays of light slipping through the gaps between leaves, created a scene both vast and beautiful.

Eda felt a sense of nostalgia, recalling the desert where she once roamed freely. In contrast to this calm, peace was something people would always seek, no matter who — or what — they were. She found herself thinking that what she had done before had been wrong, and yet, at the same time, she felt fortunate that she had been able to change without having to pay a price. Glancing toward Bezs, a thought crossed her mind — whether the path she was walking now truly held any meaning at all.

The deeper they went into the forest, the more something began to feel off. A sudden presence, something akin to fear, pressed down on Bezsmyslenoe. His innate sensitivity to what was yet to come stirred within him.

- “Hey Eda, could you send out your clones to scout the area?”

- “Huh? You’re scared? What’s there to be afraid of in this forest... It's not like we're scared of some dangerous animals, right? Did you feel something?”

Bezs remained silent, but the unease within him refused to settle. Why was there no movement in this forest, other than its almost mesmerizing beauty? It was enough to make one cautious.

Before long, the Moon Shrine stood right before them. The faint flicker of the light at its peak made Bezs feel a slight sense of doubt and unease. He wasn’t alone… was he? As he stepped onto the stairs, a quiet anxiety lingered.

- “Edaa! Send your clones to keep guard outside. If anything happens, at least we'll have a chance to defend ourselves...”

Eda clicked her tongue loudly:

- “Haha hey Bezs, you think I’m weak or what? Haha... You do know my strength better than anyone. Aren’t I protecting you? Fine then.”

Eda had always hesitated when it came to her own power, as it had once caused trouble in the past. Now, having the chance to use that very power to become something meaningful to others felt like something she could entrust herself to. Two illusory figures appeared at once and moved outside. They even talked among themselves, casually. Seeing that, Bezs felt somewhat reassured.

The Moon Shrine held many confidential documents — things that ordinary people rarely had any interest in. Bezs felt a bit discouraged, thinking that this place would likely contain nothing but useless information such as “How the Moon orbits the Earth” or “Did you know there were once three moons?” It seemed that, as always, the first place one searched would never yield what they were truly looking for.

The two of them began searching through the ruins.

- “The Suicidal Moon?! How the Crescent Moon Nourishes Agony..?!” - Bezs

- “Strange... This isn't the kind of stuff that should normally exist, right?” - Eda

Inside those eerie books, there wasn’t a single line of text. The pages were drenched in a pitch-black color, and a foul stench rose from them.

- “Fuck, It smells like something rotting. Like shit.” - Eda

Bezs’s earlier unease had not been without reason. His sensitivity to what lay ahead allowed him to sense whether something would turn out well… or not. Books bearing titles tied to suffering and despair filled most of what they found. Meaningless. Every page was stained with that same unnatural black, along with the stench of decay. Bezs felt a chill run through him.

- “Hey!! Where are my clones…? I can’t feel them anymore, what the hell...” - Eda

Bezs looked toward the entrance. The sky had turned pitch black — clearly not night, but as if something had covered it. And when he looked closely, where the sun should have been, there was… an eye, staring directly at them.

A wave of dizziness struck him. The anxiety within surged violently, as if gnawing at his very flesh. His limbs grew numb. Beside him, Eda stood in confusion and unease. Bezs felt his awareness fading, his sense of direction slipping away, as though he were slowly sinking into darkness.
