*“The Diamond of Hope contains droplets of refined power from the final age of the ancient Black Sun Dynasty, unlawfully drawing energy from the Black Sun without the permission of the gods. The Black Sun Dynasty remained powerful for centuries because it stole the abundant power of the Black Sun itself.”* ~ The Great Cataclysm of the Sun-Moon Gorge.

Beneath the evening streetlamps, the streets of Asterfall had lit up at some unnoticed moment. Mixed with the wind came the rustling sound of paper pages, Bezs hurriedly read through the explanations inside his notebook, a notebook that looked worn and torn, yet the scraps of paper within seemed to have been carefully torn from other documents and pasted in place with patience, neatness, and remarkable completeness. With such an abundant source of power, surely there must be a way to make use of it, perhaps it could be useful? The knowledge that Bezs had once studied and committed to memory came flooding back one after another. There was the Mirror Crescent Formation, an arcane formation that Atom had eagerly shared with the group a long time ago, green and crimson radiance emitted from the red and blue diamonds, containing immense power whenever illuminated, creating a point of intersection between the two circles of light. Only when an enormous source of Pyro elemental energy was fired into that point of intersection would the chaotic energy sealed within spread outward, forming the Mirror Crescent Matrix, something capable of resisting the very nature of memory manipulation and intrusion.

The wind droned mournfully from somewhere beyond the road, threaded with the sound of a distant howl, Bezs's lengthy and detailed explanations were sometimes difficult to follow, yet the look in Ray's eyes suggested that he already knew all of this. The price of forbidden things was far too great, something Ray understood better than anyone. The evening wind hummed past both of their ears, from the Aszha Forest, leaves drifted across the sky before falling onto the empty road beneath the dreamy crimson-pink glow of sunset. Ray looked toward the shining sun, yet within it, he felt as though something bleak and sorrowful still lingered there.

At some point, the city had become quiet, the market stalls had already been neatly packed away along the roadside, the once-crowded flow of pedestrians had dwindled to only a few women carrying bundles of vegetables and leftover cuts of meat from a prosperous day of trade. The crying of a baby came from a mother gently comforting the child in her arms. The rustling of leaves still remained, accompanied by the eager and hurried turning of pages from the young researcher.

More than twenty minutes had already passed, and the ground was now covered by a disorderly pile of books, some containing records of ordinary creatures, others discussing rare astronomical phenomena, clearly, no small amount of effort had gone into gathering and working through them. Ray had stood waiting for quite a while, looking at the pile of books Bezs had pulled from his bag, a brief feeling of hopelessness passing through him as he realized that none of them seemed to contain the information he had been hoping for. Then suddenly, Bezs's voice rang out clearly:

*“The Blue Diamond, despair... comforts the Child of the Moon, witnesses ten thousand rebirths...”* ~ The Angel's Sorrowful Diary

With unmistakable excitement in his voice, Bezs quickly read through the explanation before hurriedly flipping to another page. A small note attached near the back of one of the books carried a line of writing:

_"Hung inside the Sacred Bell at the heart of the Thousand Pillars Sanctuary, near the summit of the highest mountain in the Yakh Range."_

- “Here it is!” - Bezs
    

A place abandoned by the world for centuries, buried within a land of ice and snow. Bezs only knew that it was far away, impossibly high, a place he had never seen with his own eyes, known only through photographs and written records. Weariness briefly surfaced in Ray's eyes as he let out a sigh:

- "That place... Are you sure you wanna go there?" - Ray curled his lips slightly, unable to hide his distaste.
    

- "It's the only way to deal with him, I think." - Bezs
    

- "Yeah... I was honestly hoping you wouldn't find it... I don't really want to watch you get your neck snapped." - Ray
    

- "You're strange.." - Bezs
    

Bezs stood up, holding the book in his hand, pacing back and forth while thinking. Mixed with a trace of panic brought on by the urgency of the situation, the thought of crossing more than two thousand kilometers and climbing a mountain over four thousand meters high felt almost impossible.

- "Climbing gear, a support backpack, anti-slip mountaineering boots, two flashlights, spare batteries, a medical kit, ten kilograms of rations, and electrolyte supplies... Two thousand kilometers, flying at one hundred and fifty kilometers per hour would take eight hours. Four thousand meters of elevation then..." - Bezs
    
Before he could finish speaking, a loud, bitter finger snap came from Ray.
A crimson mirror slowly opened before them in less than five seconds, the heat radiating from it causing Bezs to instinctively step backward. Its surface seemed bottomless, reflecting the image of a long corridor lined on both sides with towering marble pillars. Light from the ornate patterns along the mirror's frame glowed faintly beneath the corner of a dark alley illuminated by streetlamps.

The focused expression on Bezs's face gradually changed into surprise, his eyes slowly widening as he stared into the abyss within the mirror, the image of the place they needed to reach appeared directly before him, while the blazing red light pouring from it seemed intent on burning everything it touched.

- "Oh.. I forgot." - Bezs

- "Forgot what?" - Ray turned to look at Bezs.

An invisible unease emerged from nowhere, together with a faint breeze brushing against the back of his neck, forming a nameless fear inside Bezs's mind, he hesitated, taking another small step backward from the mirror:

- "But I'm... not rea..." - Bezs

- "No" - Ray

Without a moment's hesitation, Ray grabbed Bezs by the back of his coat as if lifting a basket and hurled him straight into the mirror. The mirror's surface let out a low, melodic hum the instant every strand of Bezs's flesh touched it and slowly passed through it like a curtain, then he began drifting aimlessly into a void, a place where its bottomless abyss burned crimson as though it wished to swallow time itself and all living things, the silence within spreading through every corner of that mysterious dimension while Bezs continued hurtling straight toward the white light projecting the image of their destination, a thin blue crack opening faintly above his head amidst a blood-red expanse, he shot rapidly toward the other side of the mirror.

About two minutes later, Bezs was thrown out and slammed back-first into a marble pillar carved with intricate patterns running along its length.

- "Ouch!"
    

Bezs rose to his feet, and before him stood tens of thousands of pure white stone pillars arranged in flawless rows. Vertical grooves ran along the bodies of the pillars in perfect alignment with one another, they were tall, rising straight toward their summits, free of elaborate ornamentation, shaped only with strength and precision. The white pillars stretched onward into the distance like a corridor, giving the impression of a vast painting extending endlessly before his eyes. Beneath his feet lay a floor paved with smooth ivory marble slabs, cracks spread across the stone ground and climbed up the pillars themselves, among them stood pillars whose tops had long since broken away, a thin layer of snow resting upon the stone floor even though part of the plaza's roof shielded it from the sky above. Bezs stood frozen in awe, overwhelmed by the grandeur of the structure before him.

Not a single soul could be seen upon the snowy mountain, only the sound of ancient pine branches trembling beneath the cold wind. Pine needles brushed against one another, creating a rustling sound like tens of thousands of overlapping whispers. Mixed among them was the delicate chiming of wind bells, ethereal and unreal, echoing from somewhere deep within the sanctuary. The howling wind swept through the sea of white pillars, blending with the bells and the whispering needles, forming a strange harmony, until the sound resembled a choir of angels singing somewhere far beyond the world, chanting a pilgrimage hymn of rebirth.

Behind Bezs, a deep crimson light suddenly flared once more, radiating molten warmth that pushed back the bitter cold of the blizzard atop Mount Yakh. The light of the red mirror illuminated an entire hall of countless pillars stretching far into the depths before vanishing, leaving behind only the wind as it continued to howl between the stone columns.

Bezs took his first steps forward, nervous, excited, yet carrying a faint fear and unease somewhere deep inside him. Before he could even voice his concerns, Ray warned him:

- "This is the Sea of Pillars. We'll go deeper inside through two more chambers, that'll take us to the place you mentioned. Remember what I told you earlier, do your best not to look directly at the veiled statues inside, no matter what happens!"
    

Just like before, Bezs's anxiety still lingered somewhere inside, slowly gnawing at him second by second for reasons he could not understand. As he walked, he looked up at the endless rows of pillars standing in perfect alignment on either side, the cracks running along their surfaces filled with patches of white snow, some of the pillars stained with a strange gray discoloration, the flow of those colors forming random streaks that somehow remained eerily aligned from pillar to pillar, while the darkness of the Sea of Pillars itself dulled the crimson fabric of Ray's coat until it appeared almost gray. After a long while of walking and observing, the doorway leading into the next chamber finally came into view. Following Ray's example, Bezs instinctively raised the palm of his left hand before his eyes and lowered his gaze toward the floor.

Within his limited field of vision, he could still make out rows of stone statues standing along both sides of the chamber, draped in flowing stone robes and bearing enormous wings. Some covered their faces with their hands, while others knelt with clasped hands in solemn devotion, facing the path upon which the two of them walked. The nervousness and unease had grown stronger now, running through Bezs's chest and along his spine, while the delicate chiming of wind bells seemed to draw closer with every step. Even so, the sound of Ray's footsteps directly ahead of him brought him at least a small measure of comfort.

The path beneath his feet continued straight through the corridor, before splitting into two separate directions, each leading toward a doorway waiting at the very end. Ray gestured to Bezs, indicating that there were no more statues beyond this point.

- "Let's split up and search. I'll take the left side, just don't look at them. I'll be there the moment you're in danger, don't worry!" - Ray
    

The reassurance he had felt only moments ago seemed to be severed at once, leaving behind nothing but overwhelming fear. Sweat began forming drop by drop despite the freezing winds and snow that never ceased pouring into the sanctuary. Bezs made his way toward the doorway ahead of him, still carefully shielding his eyes with the palm of his right hand and keeping his head lowered as he stepped through the doorway into a vast hall.

Only a moment ago, the rushing wind had been pouring from the Sea of Pillars into the chamber. Now it had fallen strangely silent.

Every sound of his footsteps struck against his chest like the blow of a hammer. His heartbeat had become painfully clear, mixing together with the chiming bells and the sound of his own uneven breathing. He walked for what felt like an eternity, then suddenly collided with a pane of blue glass directly before him, startling him, yet his hand remained firmly raised over his eyes. Carefully, he glanced downward toward his feet.

In the reflection beneath him, he saw it.

A statue hung upside down from a rope disappearing into the ceiling behind him, slightly to his right. Both of its hands concealed its face completely, only a narrow slit remained. Behind it, a single black eye stared directly at Bezs.

The pounding of his heart felt as though it were about to burst from his chest. Sweat soaked through him. Waves of panic crawled along Bezs's back and ribs. He squeezed his eyes shut as his breathing grew heavier and heavier. The fear crawled up his spine, climbed his throat, and settled beneath his skin, wrapping around him as though it wished to swallow him whole. That fear climbed upward along his throat, causing one or two tears to spill helplessly onto the floor. After several desperate breaths, he broke into a panicked sprint straight ahead toward the doorway leading to the next chamber, while his hand remained clamped over his eyes.

Passing through the next doorway, Bezs stumbled and fell, his tear-filled eyes still tightly shut..

- "What's wrong with you?" - ???
    

The sound of Ray's voice directly in front of him was like medicine, dissolving the fear that had been building inside him all this time. Bezs slowly opened his eyes, his breathing still ragged from before.

Ray stood before him upon a staircase, holding a blue diamond that emitted a mysterious azure glow. Around the chamber, the stone statues all faced the cradle behind Ray. Their hands were clasped together, their eyes raised toward the opening in the ceiling above, reverent and solemn, their deep black eyes dark and endless. Hanging above the cradle was a small bell, no larger than a child's toy.

Ray stepped forward.

- "Come here, look.. here..! Take.. it!" - Ray?
    

In Ray's hand was the Diamond of Despair, the very diamond Bezs had spent so much time researching and recording within his worn notebooks. Despite Ray's pale gray skin, crossed with bluish-gray veins running across his face, Bezs felt a surge of relief. He hurried forward, slowly extending his hand.

Then a booming voice shouted from somewhere beyond the chamber:

- "DON'T TOUCH IT!" - ???

Before Bezs could even recognize whose voice it was, a crimson mirror suddenly appeared beneath his feet. He fell, and plunged straight into the crimson abyss within the mirror.

Within the crimson void as before, everything was silent. Bezs's heartbeat still echoed in heavy pulses, accompanied by his ragged breathing, the only sounds breaking the stillness of the mysterious dimension within the mirror. After a long while, a white light began to spread, swallowing his entire field of vision. Dizziness washed over him. As the light gradually faded and the scene before him became clear, Bezs silently looked around and found himself inside a cold room.

His limbs were numb and trembling. Sweat still streamed down his body, soaking his shirt. His heart continued to pound violently, each beat sending a sharp pain through his chest. Bezs swallowed dryly. His throat still felt scraped raw, as though sand had been dragged across it. His arms and legs felt weak. Both of his hands, planted behind him for support, trembled uncontrollably, as though a current of electricity were still running through every strand of flesh. Bezs let out a few broken sobs like a frightened child, then instinctively wiped away a tear still clinging to the corner of his eye.

Bezs stood up. 
The room was familiar. A single glance at the bed behind him and the photograph hanging nearby was enough for him to realize that he was inside Ray's room, neat and orderly as always. The air conditioner was still humming softly. He slowly closed his eyes. The image of the upside-down statue, with its slightly parted hands, continued flickering before his vision, causing him to shudder.

A familiar crimson light suddenly flashed before him. 
Another mirror appeared, illuminating the darkened room. Its flames cast warmth throughout the space, pushing back the chill pouring endlessly from the air conditioner that had never been switched off. Ray burst out of the mirror amidst a shower of sparks before it closed behind him. Dust still clung to his soaked crimson suit, remnants of the sanctuary. His expression was grave.

- "I TOLD YOU NOT TO LOOK AT THE STATUES!" - Ray
    

Still breathing unevenly, Bezs hurriedly recounted everything he had experienced within the silent chamber. He insisted that he had followed Ray's instructions. He had kept his eyes covered the entire time and had never once intended to look directly at the statue. Yet somehow he had still encountered a blue mirror that seemed to have already been waiting there, and the upside-down statue. The blue diamond had been right before his eyes. It sounded impossible. After a few moments of silence, Ray replied:

- "No. All I saw was you surrounded by about ten statues. They were seconds away from snapping your neck because you were about to touch them. There wasn't any diamond there at all."
    

Even after thoroughly searching the chambers beyond, Ray had been unable to find any trace of the blue diamond. Nobody spoke. The room sat in silence beneath the cold air pouring from the air conditioner that had been running for two straight days.

- "I'm going to take a shower.." - Ray
    

Bezs walked over and quietly sat down at Ray's desk. He glanced up at the clock on the wall. Tick. Tick. Tick.
It was already nearing seven in the evening.
Then his eyes drifted toward the pile of documents he had only recently pulled from his bag. There were only four days left until the Moonwatch Festival, and the thesis regarding the Crimson Sky Phenomenon was still little more than a blank page. The sound of running water echoed from the bathroom. Outside the window, darkness had already settled across the world. Wild tree branches still swayed beyond the glass with a soft rustling sound. The cries of an infant. The mournful calls of Lyre birds drifting into the room. The sweat on his forehead had finally dried, yet his limbs still felt weak and hollow. Absentmindedly, he opened his laptop. Exhausted. Worried. Yet still forced to finish the thesis before presenting it to everyone at the upcoming Moonwatch Festival.

The cursor remained motionless. On the Word document, only a few hundred words had been written. Bezs stared at the title for several minutes. Then he looked again toward the ticking clock... It was already past seven.
Bezs raised his hand and began typing the first lines beneath an introduction that had been abandoned halfway through.

- _"Hello! I am Bezsmyslenoe, a classical scholar from a distant land. This is Eda, who will be responsible for explaining this unusual astronomical phenomenon, on the occasion of a once-in-five-hundred-years event..."_ - Thesis on the Crimson Sky.
    
The wind murmured softly, pushing branches against the windowpane in gentle rhythmic taps. The clattering of keys echoed through the quiet room, mingling with the sound of running water from the bathroom. Lines of text appeared one after another upon the screen, sometimes deleted, then written again. Bezs rested his chin on his hand and stared at the monitor for a few moments before continuing to type. Beyond the window, the sky had long since gone dark, leaving only the streetlights of Asterfall casting their glow upon the trees swaying in the wind.

- _"Hello everyone, I will be explaining the Red Sky Phenomenon. I am.. Eda..."_ - Thesis on the Crimson Sky.
    
The clattering of the keyboard was suddenly interrupted by a long burst of harsh static erupting from the television across from the desk. Bezs froze. He lifted his head and looked toward it.

"Beep beep beep... beeeeep... beeeeep... beeeeep... Beep beep beep..."

The distorted image upon the television gradually sharpened. He saw Atom's room. An Eda was struggling desperately, trying to free herself from something black that looked exactly like her. It wore a grin stretched all the way to its ears. Black shadows slowly merged together, spreading through the flesh of Eda's arm...
