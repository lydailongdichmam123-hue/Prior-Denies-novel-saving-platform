“LET.. ME.. IN!!” - ...

“LET… ME… INN!!” - ...

The frantic, hysterical pounding erupted from outside Bezs’s door, each strike slamming into the wood like it was trying to tear its way through. Half-asleep, half-conscious, he felt the room turn unnaturally cold. A dim, blood-red glow spread across the ceiling and walls, as if something alive was slowly bleeding through them—thick, viscous, dripping downward toward the floor. His eyes locked onto the door. That voice— It didn’t sound like someone begging to be let in. Each repetition twisted further, warping, breaking apart—layered with countless other voices, fused together into something grotesque and inhuman. Bezs panicked. He tried to lift his arm—but nothing happened. His body trembled violently, numb and unresponsive. His heart slammed against his ribs like it was trying to escape his chest. Every inch of him felt unbearably heavy, as if something unseen was pressing down on him, crushing him into the bed. His limbs no longer belonged to him. They were there, but not his. All he could do was stare. The voice persisted. Still at the door. Still calling. It sounded like a man... but not quite. As if a man’s voice had been shredded apart and stitched back together with dozens of others, all speaking at once through the same throat. The doorknob began to twist violently. Rattling. Grinding. The sharp, metallic creaking bored straight into his skull like nails being hammered into bone.

“LET. ME. INN!!” - ...

“LET. ME. INNN!!!” - ...

Something... Something was seeping through the gap beneath the door. A thick, black liquid. It spread slowly across the floor, crawling outward like it was alive.

Then—The smell hit him. Rot. Putrefaction. The unmistakable stench of flesh left to decay. It flooded the room, suffocating, invasive, forcing its way into his lungs. The pounding didn’t stop. The voice didn’t stop. It only grew louder. Closer. More distorted.

The temperature dropped even further, the air turning sharp and biting, as if the entire room was being dragged toward something frozen and dead.

Bezs’s breathing shattered into shallow gasps. Terror swallowed him whole.

He squeezed his eyes shut, clinging desperately to the only thought he had left—

_This isn’t real._

_This has to be a dream._

. . .

“Bezs!!”

“Bezs!! Hey... it’s Ray. Bezs!”

He jolted awake. His entire body was drenched in sweat, his face still twisted in the lingering shape of fear that hadn’t quite left yet. His limbs felt numb, hollow—like something inside him hadn’t woken up along with the rest of his body, like the nightmare was still there, still gnawing at him from within. Ray’s voice from outside the door felt distant at first, then slowly pulled him back, thin and fragile, barely enough to hold onto. At least… the pounding was gone. The voice was gone. For now.

Bezs staggered forward and pulled the door open.

- “Ray… what is it…?”

Sunlight flooded in, blindingly bright—too bright. It pierced into his eyes almost violently, as if the world outside was trying too hard to convince him that everything was normal. It wasn’t.

Ray looked at Bezs, and his initial burst of energy faltered. His expression became complicated—a tangled mix of pity and a deep, simmering dread.

- “Bezs… are you alright? You had a nightmare, didn’t you?”

His usual calm was gone, stripped away completely. What remained was quieter, but far more unsettling—a restrained fear, barely held together, leaking through his eyes as he stared into Bezs’s hollow, exhausted gaze.

- “I came to tell you. Atom and Eda are waiting. Head over to her room… I won’t be joining. I have some old business to settle, meet you all later. While you get ready, I’ll head over there first to say a few words to them before I leave.”

Alone in his own room, Bezs felt a creeping unease; it seemed his sanctuary was no longer safe, and even the place he called home felt uncomfortable. And Ray’s expression just now... what was that about? Confused and heavy with melancholy, he gathered his things to head to Atom’s room. Though it was only a few steps away, a crushing weight still pressed down on both his feet.

As he looked toward the end of the hallway, it stretched unnaturally long, far beyond its true length. The overhead lights were dead; instead, the sun bled across the walls, casting long, skeletal shadows that resembled reaching bones. Yet, despite the bright sunlight, a phantom chill clung to the back of his neck, unfading... as if something were still standing right behind him."

Ahead, Ray stepped out of Atom’s room. He glanced back at Bezs... his eyes still clouded with that same unspoken dread. Then, without a word, Ray’s form ignited into a sudden flicker of flame. He vanished into the air, leaving nothing but a few motes of black ash drifting to the floor.

Upon reaching Atom’s door, he was met by the brilliant glow of the overhead lights, illuminating a lavish feast prepared by Atom and Eda. For a fleeting moment, Bezs felt the warmth of a family—a sensation born from the presence of his friends.

To one side, an Eda sat perched on a chair with her legs crossed, thumbing through a book as she struggled to decipher the script within. Beside her, another Eda stood motionless, her eyes fixed on the pot of Coconut Water Hotpot with a look of ravenous hunger. In Eda’s hands lay a tattered yet thick volume; its cover, crafted from the hide of some unknown beast, was worn and frayed. Embossed upon it were the words: “The Ritual of Moonfall.”

Atom approached him with a plate of pastries. She smiled, but for the first time, the warmth in her eyes felt fragile.

- “Morning, Bezs! I learned these recipes from Ray… I didn’t steal them, I swear hehe.”

The table was laden with a sumptuous spread; the fragrance of honey and savory broth wafted through the air, momentarily dulling the horrors that had tormented Bezs the night before. It had been months since he last sat down to such a complete meal. A wave of satisfaction washed over him, followed by a flicker of true happiness—the feeling that he finally had a family, a real home. It was a warmth he wished he could never forget.

- “Yeah, Bezs, hahaha. Sit down and eat. You look like you’re about to starve to death.” - Eda

The group gathered around the feast, savoring the food while reminiscing about days gone by. The rhythmic _tick-tock_ of the pendulum clock on the wall paced the room as Eda tossed the tattered book onto a corner of the sofa. An hour slipped away. The meal was delicious and cozy, yet an inexplicable strangeness lingered in the air.

Throughout the breakfast, Atom never took her eyes off Bezs’s weary gaze. Slowly, her cheerful expression began to wither, her skin turning ashen as her smile faded. Her face became clouded with a sudden, mounting horror, causing the room to fall into an unnatural silence—despite the sound of two Edas still eating beside them.

The food in Bezs’s mouth, which only a moment ago had been rich and flavorful, suddenly turned bland and hollow, before gradually hardening into an inexplicable, stinging bitterness.

- “Hey, Witch… you good?” - Eda asked Atom.

- “Inside your eyes…” Atom’s voice was grave, laced with an underlying panic. “It feels like something… is looking back at me.”

- “Aren’t I the one looking at you?” - Bezs countered.

- “No, I mean… deep within your eye, there is another eye…” - Atom M.

A phantom chill raced down the spines of all three. The feast was over. Eda merged with her clone after that, her playful presence vanishing. The jarring realization that something was deeply wrong with Bezs caused Atom to instinctively glance toward the tattered book resting on the sofa.

It was chilling; despite being a Witch of Hell, her vast power seemed to shrink, becoming pitifully small before the fate Bezs was about to endure. The fear radiating from someone who had once been a villain made him realize… his destiny was about to collide with the brutal cruelty of the truth.

- “This is a mark,” Atom stated, her voice trembling. “The eye within… it is the sign that you have been cursed and possessed by an Angel...”

In the back of his mind, Bezs knew that angels were supposed to be the protectors of humanity. But that no longer seemed to matter. Was it not true that this entity had been seeing everything he saw, witnessing his every moment through his very own eyes?

Eda remained perplexed, a look of confusion clouding her face. Sensing that both Atom and Bezs understood something she didn’t, she felt a wave of inadequacy—as if she were weak and useless to her friends.

- “I really don’t get any of this... Haha, do I really look so stupid right now. What’s with this book? And what exactly is happening?” Eda asked, her voice trailing off.

. . .

The book, “Ritual of Moonfall” was in truth a collection of myths detailing the creation of a Seraphim by the Mother Angel in the Heavenly Realm:

- “_I grant thee strength and life; go forth to the brink of darkness and the abyss. I grant thee power and status; let go and cast thyself down to balance the sins and melancholy of all living beings. I grant thee infinite rebirth; protect the fragile lives of arrogant humanity!_” - Naberius

His lifespan was destined to be exactly five hundred years. From the moment of his rebirth to the end of his life cycle, his psyche gradually shifted. From a pure, radiant angel, he slowly descended into melancholy; the closer he drew to the end of his 500-year term, the more consumed he became by gloom and sorrow. Upon reaching his end, he would hurl himself into the Edge of the World—a bottomless abyss shrouded in eternal shadow, a place where no mortal dared to tread—ending his existence in disgrace and despair.

- “_Power of the moon... O, Angel of the radiant crescent; O, thou who must fall for the arrogance of man. Cast thyself into eternal exile, to cleanse the transgressions of humanity._” - Naberius

Somehow, at the close of his five-hundred-year cycle, the sky of the mortal realm would be stained a deep, sanguine red.

- “_O, Angel beneath the bleak moon; O, Angel beneath the bleak moon. My gratitude to thee, who hast bathed thy body in blood to immerse thyself in the atmosphere of thy own mourning... Ledeniel._” - Vedrfolnir

. . .

The terrifying coincidence left Atom, Eda, and Bezs suffocating under the weight of what had unfolded—from the contents of the book to the eye in the sky, and Bezs’s grotesque nightmare. The joyful laughter at the feast had died away unnoticed, leaving only a forced smile on Eda’s youthful face and a heavy silence from Atom.

- “Bezs, I think you should leave this place. Go to the city; Ray is likely there. Your nightmare last night shows that this place is no longer safe for you.” - Atom M.

- “Atom and I will stay here to look after your room, Bezs. Don’t forget me, haha! Good luck!” - Eda.

Bezs looked toward the dirt path leading into Aszha Forest, the road to the city where everyone would gather for the Moon-Watching Festival and prayers in just a few weeks. With regret, he glanced back at his empty room—the place he had called home for a year, now no longer safe.

Walking along the gravel path, he gazed toward the distant horizon. The weeping willows lined both sides of the trail, the forest as cold as ever, reflecting the sunlight filtering through the canopy. Yet, the unease of being alone clung to him just as it had the day before. The forest held no wild beasts, no monsters; there was only the sound of infants crying from every direction—the Lyrebirds perched upon the willow branches, looking down at Bezs with their mouths agape. Along with them was the whistling wind and the willows swaying in the breeze.

The path was riddled with damp patches and uneven, jagged earth. Half an hour passed unnoticed; only fifteen minutes remained until he would clear the forest. The silhouette of the Moon Shrine remained visible, flickering in and out of sight to his left.

The weeping willows continued to sway, the wind hissed, and the leaves—once a vibrant green—turned grey, then faded into an inexplicable blur. Dusk was beautiful, yet cold and lonely. Bezs’s dizziness returned, striking in the heart of the forest... He realized the foliage was no longer lush; a leaden grey had smothered the entirety of Aszha Forest. The sky was devoid of clouds, gradually turning a strange shade of ash. The dizziness was dull and persistent, and a sharp chill bit into his collarbone, racing down his spine.

He looked straight to the end of the road, where the two rows of weeping willows seemed to stretch into infinity. A white silhouette, at first faint and then vivid, appeared before him. A tall man, perhaps 1.8 meters tall, stood with his back turned. The figure suddenly turned toward Bezs, wearing an elegant and sophisticated white suit with a white rose pinned to the breast pocket, a 70s-style white belt, and a tailcoat flowing past his knees. His hair was white, topped with a white crown that radiated a halo—an aura of majesty so terrifying it was overwhelming. His hands, which had been covering his face, slowly spread apart and dropped, revealing a blindfold made of fine silk. Beneath the blindfold, three or four rows of black tears carved paths down his cheeks. His face was pale and ashen, his mouth set in a deep sorrow; the blindfold hid eyes that had seemingly endured countless invisible agonies.

Bezs’s limbs suddenly turned rigid, stripped of the strength to move. His knees were forced down by an unseen power, his back straightened, his arms and head hanging limp against his will. He could not move; he could not scream for help. The stranger began to move—not by walking, but by gliding slowly toward Bezs. His movement against the fallen leaves on the trail created a bone-like crackling sound. As he drew near, the black tears falling from his cheeks splattered onto the ground, bringing with them the suffocating stench of rotting carrion that surged into Bezs’s nostrils.

In a deathly silence, the voice of a man—distorted and blended with a choir of different voices—rose unnervingly:

- “Truth—the origin of all pain, O thou who walkest upon the path illuminated by my light in the heavens.” — Ledeniel

Bezs’s body was weak, frozen like a stone statue awaiting death. His eyes burned with a stinging heat as black tears began to fall to the earth. The stench of decay erupted from them, sending a surge of nausea through him, but his mouth could not move; his vocal cords felt as though they had been torn apart. A drop of black liquid fell into his mouth. Bitter and metallic...

The overlapping whispers of many voices clawed at his ears, warped and incomprehensible, before gradually shifting into screams that threatened to puncture his eardrums. The liquid flowing from his eyes felt as if it were burning his face; it began to flood into his mouth, his throat searing with pain. He vomited the thick, black substance, the smell of death becoming even more pungent and horrifying. He was forced to endure it all in that rigid, paralyzed posture. Pain and panic tore violently through his body from within.

- “O thou who walkest my path, thou art worthy to know all the truths of the world, and worthy to feel the pain they bring.” — Ledeniel

- “A night of the moon, despair; the dying moonlight shines upon the truth to unveil agony. The smile of the sage fades before ten thousand blades of truth piercing his heart. Shed thy tears of denial, and cleanse thy sorrows, O miserable human.” — Ledeniel

The piercing screams of ten thousand souls continued to tear at Bezs. The pain from the black liquid, as if trying to destroy his very eyes, eventually drained him completely. He fell into a coma, losing all consciousness.

. . .

- “Let the truth hollow out thy entrails, and thy eyes as well, O miserable human. Thou art worthy to pay the price for the dark secrets of eternity.” — Ledeniel
